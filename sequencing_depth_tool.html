Meta-CD: Metagenomic Sequencing Coverage and Depth Calculator

This tool is designed for species‑specific metagenomic evaluation (post sequencing and community processing), allowing researchers to estimate the sequencing coverage, required depth, minimum detectable abundance, and MAG recovery potential for an individual microbial species; based on its genome size, relative abundance, sequencing depth, and sample DNA quantity.

Input Parameters

What these inputs mean:
  • Relative abundance (%): The proportion of total metagenomic reads belonging to the species of interest after community processing.
  • Genome size (Mb): The genome length of the species you are evaluating, in megabases.
  • Sequencing depth (Gb): The total sequencing generated for the entire sample (community-level depth).
  • Target coverage (×): The desired sequencing coverage for this species; used to calculate required depth.
  • Target coverage for minimum RA (×): The coverage threshold used to compute the minimum detectable relative abundance.
  • DNA quantity (ng): Total DNA extracted from the sample; limits the maximum effective sequencing depth.

Coverage From Sequencing Depth

This section uses relative abundance, genome size, sequencing depth, and DNA quantity. Coverage represents how many times each base of the genome is sequenced on average. Higher coverage increases confidence in detection, reduces false negatives, and improves assembly quality. DNA quantity acts as a cap on effective sequencing depth, limiting achievable coverage regardless of nominal depth.

Required Sequencing Depth for Target Coverage

This section uses target coverage, relative abundance, genome size, and DNA quantity. It calculates how much sequencing is needed to reach a desired coverage level for a given organism. Lower abundance organisms require disproportionately more sequencing to reach the same coverage. DNA quantity limits the maximum achievable depth, so extremely high target coverage may be impossible.

Minimum Relative Abundance Required

This section uses target coverage, genome size, sequencing depth, and DNA quantity. It determines the minimum abundance at which an organism can be detected or assembled at a desired coverage. Lower sequencing depth or larger genome size increases the required abundance. DNA quantity limits the effective depth, raising the minimum abundance needed for detection.

How These Calculations Work

1. Bases sequenced for the species
Total sequencing depth is measured in gigabases (Gb), where 1 Gb = 109 bases. If a species has relative abundance A (e.g., 1% = 0.01), then the number of bases sequenced for that species is: 

Species bases = D × 10⁹ × A

2. Genome size conversion
Genome size G is entered in megabases (Mb), where 1 Mb = 106 bases. 

Genome bases = G × 10⁶

3. Coverage formula
Coverage is defined as the number of sequenced bases divided by the genome size: 

Coverage = (D × 10⁹ × A) / (G × 10⁶)
         = (D × 1000 × A) / G

This is the standard definition of sequencing coverage used in genomics and metagenomics.

4. Required sequencing depth for a target coverage
Rearranging the coverage formula gives: 

Required depth (Gb) = (Coverage × G) / (1000 × A)

This is commonly used in metagenomic sampling‑depth calculations for MAG recovery.
